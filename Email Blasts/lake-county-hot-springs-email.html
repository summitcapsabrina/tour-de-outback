Soak It In: A Rider's Guide to Lake County's Hot Springs

By Dave Tucker

For a few years, the spring just north of Lakeview went by the Neon Cowboy. This year, under new ownership, it reclaimed the name it's carried since 1923: Hunter's Hot Springs, home to Old Perpetual, the only continuously erupting geyser in the Pacific Northwest.

It's a good excuse to take stock, because Hunter's isn't the only hot water in Lake County. This is one of the most geothermally active corners of Oregon, and for anyone rolling through it on a bike, that's not just trivia.

Warm water opens blood vessels, increases circulation, and helps flush the fatigue out of legs that just spent hours under load. It's recovery, built right into the landscape.

Sixty-seven miles northeast, a stone-walled pool sits free and first-come at the Hart Mountain campground, no cell service, no crowds, just a soak under some of the darkest skies in the country. Hunter's itself sits inside that same Oregon Outback Dark Sky Sanctuary, so a late soak comes with a Milky Way overhead. Add Fisher Hot Springs near Crump Lake and the century-old bathhouse at Summer Lake, and Lake County holds more hot water than a county this size has any right to.

Our latest blog post covers all four springs, the science behind why a soak helps a body that just rode a century, and how to build one into your own Lake County weekend.
